The problem

On a heavy-rain day, the ambulance is sent the way it goes on a dry day.

  • Who: the ambulance dispatcher, with under a minute per call.
  • Today: the nearest hospital, normal route; a flooded underpass is found on arrival.
  • Gap: navigation apps react after a road is blocked. The dispatcher needs to know before.

How it decides

A transparent score, then a real route. No black box.

1 · Flood-prone score per road

Four signals from terrain:

▸ lower than the area around it
▸ low height above sea level
▸ close to sea, creek or canal
▸ underpass

→

2 · Rain day → blocked roads

The IMD category sets how many of the most flood-prone roads count as likely to flood.

Heavy 8% · Very heavy 15% · Extremely heavy 25%

→

3 · Two routes

Fastest path to the nearest major hospital on the full road network…

…and again with the likely-to-flood roads removed.

→

4 · Decision

Same route → "not affected". Longer → "+N min", maybe a different hospital. No path → "cut off".

Why a transparent score, not machine learning? Flood reports exist for a handful of spots, not thousands. A score you can explain road by road is more honest and easier to trust in an emergency.

Why this is different

In a flood, the hard decision is not the turn. It is the hospital.
